📖 What is the Legislative Monitor?

The Legislative Monitor — Chamber of Deputies is an open-source analysis tool that measures the composition and performance of the 257 deputies of the Argentine Nation. It organizes 12 indicators in 4 dimensions (Costs and Inputs, Process Efficiency, Performance and Quality, and Transparency and Data), adds composition indicators for the Chamber and a profile with data on each deputy.

The site runs on its own server (FastAPI), which serves the pages and an API with the data. The data is updated automatically, every day, by a process that queries the official sources (see Updates).

The pages themselves are in Spanish; this manual uses the on-screen Spanish labels, with their English meaning, so you can find each item.

🎯 Goal

To promote legislative transparency and informed debate about the composition and functioning of the Chamber of Deputies, by putting official data in an accessible format for citizens, journalists and researchers.

Moving between pages

The pages share a navigation header (top right) and an inner bar (sticky, in blue) with quick links to each page's sections.

💡 On a phone, the header and the inner bar wrap into several rows when the buttons do not fit.

Search boxes and filters

1
Roster search — on the Roster page, type a name, bloc or district to filter the 257 deputies in real time.
2
Detail filters — on the Detail page, combine the search box with the bloc, district and term selectors.
3
Sorting the bloc table — on the By Bloc page, click a column header to sort from highest to lowest or the other way round.
4
Card / list view — on the Detail page, switch between visual cards and the compact table with the ▦/☰ button.

📐 How to read the indicators

Status of each indicator

Each indicator shows a label with its status:

✓ Activo (active) computed from official sources   ⚠ Estimación / auditoría (estimate / audit) approximate or reference value   🔜 Planificado v2.0 (planned) not computed yet

💡 On the Home page, when the API computes an indicator the live value is shown; otherwise the reference value (the latest published annual figure). Today the API computes NAPE, TPMP, ITC, PDA and IAP live.

Dimension I — Costs and Inputs

CodeName and formulaStatusHow to read it
CRCCost per citizen
HCDN budget / Population		✓ 2025 referenceHow much it costs each inhabitant to run the Chamber per year. 2025 reference: ARS 4,818 per inhabitant.
RPSStaff professionalization
Permanent staff / Total employees × 100		✓ 2025 referenceWhat share of staff are career employees. 2025 reference: 57.5% of 4,551 employees; the rest are advisers to blocs, deputies and committees.
IAPBudget autonomy
Executed budget / Approved budget		✓ activeThe closer to 1, the more of its own budget the Chamber executes. The live value is the execution of the latest closed fiscal year (HCDN accrued spending / current appropriation, from Presupuesto Abierto). The current year's execution, partial until year-end, is available at /api/presupuesto.

Dimension II — Process Efficiency

CodeName and formulaStatusHow to read it
TPMPBill maturation time
Σ(Committee report date − Filing date) / bills		⚠ referenceDays from a bill's filing until the committee reports on it. Fewer days = more agility. Until committee report dates from the SIL are available, a reference value is shown (105 days, historical range 30–180).
ITCCommittee work
Committee hours / Plenary hours		⚠ referenceCommittee hours per hour of plenary session. Higher = more technical work before debate. Until committee minutes are available, the historical reference value is shown (~3.5×).
NAPEEffective attendance and presence✓ activeIt has two readings, depending on where you look:
• 2025 reference value (0.27): share of deputies with perfect attendance.
• Live value on the Home page: average absence = 1 − average attendance, using the current-period attendance published by the HCDN. A value of 0.06 means that, on average, each deputy missed 6% of sessions.

Dimension III — Performance and Quality

CodeName and formulaStatusHow to read it
COLSCost per law

RLSSubstantive legislation
Substantive laws / Total laws × 100		✓ 2025 referenceWhat share of laws is real public policy rather than declarative or ceremonial. 2025 reference: 72.7% (8 of 11 ordinary laws).
ECOOversight effectiveness

🤖 The “Explicar con IA” (Explain with AI) button

Several pages have a 🤖 Explicar con IA button. It sends the data shown in that section to a language model (Claude) and displays a plain-language explanation.

⚠️ The explanation is an automatically generated reading aid: it may contain errors and does not replace the methodology. Usage is capped to control cost.

🔄 How the data is updated

Automatic daily update

Every day at 09:00 UTC (06:00 Argentina time) a GitHub Actions process runs these steps and publishes the new data to the repository:

1
Full pipeline (scraper_pipeline.py): roster, photos, attendance, bills, budget, votes, TPMP and ITC. It produces data/diputados.json, which is what the API reads.
2
Enrichment with the voting indicators (actualizar_diputados.py).
3
Monitoring agent: looks for anomalies in the new data.
4
Commit and publication of the updated data.
💡 If a source does not respond on a given day, the previous values are kept and the API says so (for example, “sin actualizar hoy” — not updated today). The date of the last update is shown in /health and in the /api/kpis metadata.

Methodological limitations

Annual reference values: CRC, RPS, COLS, RLS, IAD and TVD are annual (2025) figures. They do not change with the daily update until the next year's figure is loaded.

NEP: treats each bloc separately, without considering affinities or coalitions. A high NEP does not necessarily mean majorities are hard to build.

Attendance: measures presence at sessions, not at each vote. A deputy can be recorded as present and still have left before voting.

Bills: filed bills are counted; the source does not report which ones passed.

RLS: the substantive/declarative classification is the monitor's own and may differ from others.

Blocs: the official HCDN name at the time of the query is used; blocs may be renamed, merge or split.

📖 Quick glossary

NEP
Effective Number of Parties — how many "effective" blocs there are, weighted by size.
IF
Rae Fragmentation Index — dispersion of power among blocs (0 to 1).
IRB
Biennial Renewal Rate — percentage of seats renewed in the latest election.
IRG
Geographic Representation Index — over/under-representation of each district.
CRC
Representation Cost per Citizen — HCDN budget divided by population.
RPS
Staff Professionalization Ratio — permanent staff over total employees.
IAP
Budget Autonomy Index — executed over approved budget.
TPMP
Average Bill Maturation Time — days between filing and committee report.
ITC
Committee Work Index — committee hours per plenary hour.
NAPE
Effective Attendance and Presence Level — see the Dimension II table.
COLS
Operating Cost per Law Passed — budget divided by laws passed.
RLS
Substantive Legislation Ratio — % of public-policy laws over the total.
ECO
Oversight Effectiveness — % of AGN audits with legislative follow-up.
IAD
Document Accessibility Index — publication speed (1–5 scale).
TVD
Data Accuracy Rate — accuracy of the data captured by the monitor.
IPCV
Virtual Citizen Participation Index — planned for v2.0.
PDA
% of deputies with at least one law bill filed.
IQP
Participation in roll-call votes — votes cast over votes called.
SIL
Legislative Information System — official database of bills and votes.
OPC
Congressional Budget Office — technical budget monitoring.
HCDN
Honorable Chamber of Deputies of the Nation.
AGN
National Audit Office — external auditor of the national public sector.

🕸️ Vote-agreement network

This is the site's information graph (the Red de votos page). Each node is a deputy, shown with their name and the colour of their bloc. Each line joins them with the colleagues they voted the same way as most often in this year's roll-call votes; by default the 3 most similar (2, 3 or 5 can be chosen). Gold lines cross bloc boundaries: they show who votes alike despite belonging to different blocs.

  • Agreement between two legislators = votes in which both cast the same vote (yes, no or abstention) ÷ votes in which both voted. Absences do not count and at least 20 shared votes are required.
  • Clicking a node shows their bloc, votes cast, how often they agree with their own bloc and with the others, and which member of another bloc they agree with most. The "Highest cross-bloc agreement" list jumps to each pair.
  • "Fuera de la nómina actual" (grey) are members who voted this year but have since left the chamber.
  • Source: ArgentinaDatos roll-call records (the 120 roll-call votes of 2026, 259 deputies as of 07/11/2026). It is recomputed daily. ES / EN button to switch language.

How to read it: blocs form compact clusters; legislators sitting between two clusters, or joined by gold lines, often vote with another bloc. Voting the same way is descriptive: it implies neither a formal agreement nor anything improper.
